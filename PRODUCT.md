# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary audience is Taranto residents and concerned citizens — people checking air quality the way they'd check the weather, deciding whether to go outside, open windows, or take precautions, often with real health stakes from living near the industrial area. They are not data-literate by default: the tool has to translate a reading into a decision, not just display a number.

## Product Purpose

A community-owned air quality monitoring dashboard for Taranto. It exists to do three things at once: let residents make daily personal decisions from current readings, make the gap between "legal" (EU/EEA thresholds) and "actually safe" (WHO guidelines) impossible to miss, and give the citizen-owned sensor network a public face credible enough to be taken seriously. Success looks like people checking it habitually, and the WHO-vs-legal gap becoming a fact people repeat rather than a footnote they skip.

## Positioning

The data is citizen-owned, not official monitoring — and it refuses to let "within legal limits" pass as "safe." Both halves of that claim operate together: neither the ownership story nor the WHO-honesty stands on its own as the reason this exists instead of an official air-quality portal.

## Brand Personality

Humanistic, not clinical. The instinct to render pollution data as a sterile instrument panel is the wrong one here — warmth and plain language win over precision-for-its-own-sake. Every reading should answer "what do I do right now," in words a majority of residents can act on without translating units or thresholds themselves. Comprehension and actionability come before technical completeness.

## Anti-references

Not a corporate SaaS analytics dashboard — no generic B2B "insights" look; this isn't a product being sold. Not a government or bureaucratic portal either — it should never read as an official ARPA or comune site. It's citizen-run, and that ownership should be legible in the feel of the thing, not just stated in a footer.

## Design Principles

Explain, don't just report. Every reading pairs with what to actually do about it — a bare number is a failure state, not a finished screen.

Own the data, visibly. The interface should feel citizen-run on sight, without needing a disclaimer to earn trust.

WHO over legal, always on screen. The gap between compliant and safe is a feature to surface prominently, not a footnote for the curious.

Warm before precise. Accessible to a general public first; technical rigor is real but never gatekeeps comprehension.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA baseline. No unusual constraints confirmed yet, but the audience skews toward people with real health stakes (including older and health-affected residents), so favor generous contrast and plain language over minimum compliance where the two are in tension.
