About the Project

A Digital Truth Common

This platform is a community-owned infrastructure designed to reclaim data sovereignty for the city of Taranto. It is the result of a technical and conceptual evolution of the Aria Bene Comune project, originally launched by Linux Group Taranto—a collective of ethical hackers who deployed the city's first independent air quality sensors. While institutional data often remains a closed circuit, this platform treats information as a "Truth Common." We have expanded the original network to monitor a wider spectrum of pollutants (PM2.5, PM10, NO2, CO, and VOCs) and integrated these numbers with the lived experiences of residents. Here, technical graphs live alongside citizen reports, interviews, and photography, creating a pluralistic narrative that reflects the true reality of our territory.

Breaking the Infrastructure of Silence

For decades, the silence in Taranto has been an infrastructure as necessary to the industry as the blast furnace itself. We created this platform because the official environmental narrative has long been used to invalidate the physical reality of our citizens. As revealed in the Ambiente Svenduto trial, air quality data has often been treated as an "alienated commodity"—falsified or hidden to protect industrial production at the cost of public health. This website exists to break that silence. By making environmental data public, readable, and independent, we aim to transform the invisible "slow violence" of pollution into an undeniable public record, moving the community from being passive subjects of a poisoned landscape to becoming sovereign owners of our own story.

Reclaiming the Tools

We believe that the first step toward justice is seizing the means of information production. This project is built on a collaborative and pedagogical approach: through hands-on workshops, citizens are invited to build and maintain their own open-source sensors. This process ensures that the "right to know" is backed by the technical knowledge to measure. A central pillar of our methodology is the critique of legal limits. While official agencies often hide behind permissive EU standards that prioritize industrial "feasibility," this platform interprets data through the lens of World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines. By highlighting the gap between health-based science and industrial compromise, we link the technical data directly back to the human body, turning sensing into a collective act of resistance and care.
